Privacy Policy

Effective November 22, 2026

This policy explains what information Meetyr LLC ("Meetyr", "we", "us") collects when you use the Meetyr app and this website, how we use it, who we share it with, and the choices you have. If you have questions, email support@meetyr.com.

The short version: we collect what's needed to run a community rating app: your account, your profile, your location while you use the app (and, only if you turn on Places, People or "Let businesses know when I'm there", where you stop, who you're near and the businesses you visit; only if you help test, where you stop; during a MeetUp you both agreed to, your location goes to the person you're meeting), the ratings you give and receive, the cards your deck shows you and what you do with them, and what you share in communities. We haven't sold or shared personal information for advertising. Our website and the web version of Meetyr count visits without cookies.

Information you give us

  • Account: your email address and password, or, if you use Sign in with Apple or Google, what Apple or Google shares with us (below). Passwords are stored only in encrypted (hashed) form.
  • Sign in with Apple: Apple shares an ID for your Apple account, your email address (it can be an Apple "Hide My Email" address that forwards to you) and, the first time, your name if you choose to share it. We use the name to fill in your display name, which you can change. We also keep a token from Apple, used only to end Meetyr's access to your Apple ID when you delete your account.
  • Sign in with Google: Google shares your name, email address and profile picture, and an ID for your Google account. Meetyr gets no other access to your Google account.
  • Profile: your display name, @handle, profile photo, whether you're a person or a business, a category, and a short "About" text.
  • Ratings: the thumbs-up or thumbs-down ratings you give, and when you gave them.
  • Rating requests: when you rate a person more than 1 mile away, your confirmation that you're rating your interaction with them, and their answer.
  • Private profile: whether your profile is private (and when Private starts), and, for each rating you give, whether it was private when you gave it.
  • Communities: the communities you start or join, Meetyr's public ones included, the questions and answer options you write, your votes, your comments, your rants and reactions, the discussions and replies you write, what you share to MeetyrMaid and the ratings you give there, the photos, videos and voice clips you add, the invitations you send, get or decline, and reports about community content.
  • Community comments: in communities hosted by a person and in Meetyr's public ones, you can comment under a question while it's open, up to 140 characters, with no replies, and up to 100 comments a day. Comments can't include an @handle or, in a community hosted by a person, the full name of someone in it. The community's members see your comments with your name. You or the host can remove one. When someone reports a comment, it's hidden from everyone but you until Meetyr's team looks at it, unless that person has already used their 50 reports for the day.
  • Question photos, videos and voice clips: you can add one to a question you ask. Before a photo or video leaves your phone, the app removes its location and other saved details: photos are saved again as a smaller copy, and videos keep only their first 30 seconds, with sound. Voice clips are recorded in the app, up to 30 seconds. We store them privately, and only members of that question's community, and Meetyr's team, can open them. They stay with the question after it closes. A report hides one the same way as a comment, and Meetyr's team looks at it. Still pictures taken from each video for the automatic safety checks (below) are stored privately with it. No member can open them, and they're deleted with the video.
  • Automatic safety checks: each question photo, video and voice clip is checked automatically when it's added, and anything that fails is hidden. Microsoft's PhotoDNA compares photos, and the still pictures from videos, with known child sexual abuse images, using only a fingerprint of each picture, never the picture itself. OpenAI checks the pictures for nudity, violence and the like, and turns the sound of voice clips and videos into text to check what's said; we don't keep that text. Meetyr uses PhotoDNA technology licensed by Microsoft at no cost.
  • Safety reports: when you report a member or flag a rating you received, the reason you choose and any details you write. When you report something in a community (the community, an invitation, a question, its photo, video or voice clip, or a comment), the reason you pick, such as "It's about a person" or "It shows a person", and a copy of what you reported. We also keep a record of members you block.
  • Business verification and claims: when a business asks to be verified, claims a listing, or changes its address, the details it sends us (such as its website, a contact name and phone number, the person's role and how we can confirm them) and our record of when and how we checked. We don't collect ID documents.
  • Rants and discussions: a rant is up to 500 characters to one community you're in; members react to it with one tap and don't reply. A discussion is a title and up to 2,000 characters, with replies of up to 1,000. Neither can include an @handle or, in a community hosted by a person, the full name of someone in it. You can remove your own, and a report hides one the same way as a comment.
  • MeetyrMaid: what you share there to be rated (a title, and a photo, video or voice clip, writing, or both), and the thumbs up or down you give what others share, once each and never your own.
  • Legend drawings: if you send a photo of your drawing of a Legend, the app first removes its location and other saved details, as it does for question photos, and the photo is checked automatically (see Automatic safety checks). It's then shown among that Legend's drawings with your name, or as "A member" if your profile is private. Members can vote for each drawing once. The page shows how many votes each drawing has, never who voted, though we keep who voted for which drawing. The drawing with the most votes can become the Legend's picture, shown without your name, after Meetyr's team has looked at it. If you delete your account, your drawings and votes are removed, the Legend goes back to its original picture, and the photo file is deleted on the same schedule as a question photo's (see Keeping and deleting information).
  • Messages to us: anything you send to our support address.
  • Chats: the messages you write in dialogues and group chats, the questions you ask there and the answers you give, who they're with and when, and a group chat's topic.
  • Profile questions: the question on your profile, and your answers to others' (the person who asked sees answers without names; Meetyr keeps who wrote each).
  • Community scores: if a community's host turns them on and you join in, that you joined, each thumb you give and get, who gave it and when, and your score in that community.

Information collected when you use the app

  • Location: with your permission, your device's location while the app is open. We use it to show you people and businesses nearby, and to show you to members near you (see below). We store your most recent location for 24 hours at most, not a history of where you've been, except where you meet another member in person, if you turn on Places or People, or if you help test (see below). The record of your decks can also show roughly where you were when each was dealt (see "Your deck"). The app uses location in the background only if you turn on Places or "Let businesses know when I'm there", if you help test, or during a MeetUp (see below).
  • Home area: when you set up a personal profile, we ask where you're located (a ZIP code, or your current location). We keep only a rough area about a mile across and its town and ZIP code, never your address. While your distance is hidden, Discover shows you to people near this area instead of near where you are. You can change it in Edit profile. It, or where you signed up, also puts you in your town's local community.
  • Ratings you receive: your score and the number of ratings you've received.
  • QR code: a random code linked to your profile so others can scan it in person to rate you. Each code works for one scan; you get a new one after every scan. We keep the scan itself only for the 10 minutes the person scanning has to rate you from it; the meeting it makes is kept as described under "Bumps, touches and scans".
  • Bumps, touches and scans: when you bump phones or touch iPhones with another member while the app is open, or one of you scans the other's QR code, we record when and exactly where it happened, so the two of you can rate each other for 24 hours. Your phone's motion readings stay on your phone. We keep who you met, when and how while your account exists, and the exact place for 1 year, to look into reports of made-up meetings.
  • Invite links: when someone uses your invite link, we record who used it and when, so it can be used only once.
  • Crossed paths: when you and another member have each stayed somewhere for 10 minutes, away from home, within about a mile of each other, the two of you can rate each other for 48 hours. Each of you sees the other's card 1 to 2 hours later, or that evening, never as it happens. It counts only while "Show me near where I am" and People are on for both of you, and not when either of you is within 565 m (1,850 feet) of a family planning center. Neither of you sees where the other was. We keep who and when, and only a rough square about 400 m across, never your spot. While you both have People on, they're kept as People notes. Turning People off deletes them, except one that either of you rated from, which stays as that rating's record. Otherwise we keep who and when as for a bump, and the square for 1 year, to look into reports of made-up meetings.
  • Near a connection: when you and someone you're connected with are at the same business at the same time (from your visit notes), or your spots are saved within about 50 m of each other with Meetyr open, away from home, you each get the other's card, and rating it counts as in person. It counts only while "Show me near where I am" and People are on for both of you, never for a private member, and not when either of you is within 565 m (1,850 feet) of a family planning center. We keep who and when, and no place, except that if you first crossed paths, the note keeps that rough square, as crossed paths do (above). Turning People off deletes them, except one that either of you rated from, which stays as that rating's record. Otherwise we keep them as for a bump.
  • Camera: used only when you take a profile photo, scan a QR code, or take a photo or video for a community question. We store only the profile photo you choose and the photos and videos you post to a community.
  • Microphone: used only while you record a video or a voice clip for a community question or MeetyrMaid. Only a clip you add is stored.
  • Photo library: used only when you choose a profile photo, or a photo or video for a community question. Only the one you choose is uploaded.
  • Notifications: only if you turn them on. Visit reminders (see Places and People), a business's question after a visit, a "Remind me tomorrow" after you scan a business's code, and a "Remind me later" after you scan a person's, are set up by your phone itself. Everything else is a push notification we send (iPhone only): we save your phone's push token with your account. They tell you about new messages (who wrote, never what), connection and rating requests, MeetUps, invitations, new questions in your communities (with the community's name), new answers to your question, and when your question closes, never a message, a question or an answer. A verified business that turns them on also gets notices about its business community. You turn them on or off in Me → Notifications. Turning them off, signing out or deleting your account removes the token.
  • Location settings: each day Meetyr opens or wakes on your phone, and whenever one of them changes, the app tells us whether its location access is "Always", "While using" or off, and whether Precise Location is on, with your phone's platform, system version and app version. We keep each of these with your account, to make noticing places work, to fix problems, and to count how many members use each setting.
  • Security check: on the screens where you sign up, sign in, ask for a new code or reset your password, and when you delete your account with a password, Cloudflare Turnstile checks that you're a person, not a bot. For that check, Cloudflare receives technical details about your device and connection, such as your IP address.
  • When you use the app, we record which version of the app you're using, with what you create (ratings, votes, questions, comments, photos, videos, voice clips and reports), and each time you open the app signed in. We use it to fix problems and to know which rules applied.
  • Your deck: we keep a record of each deck we deal you in the app. It holds when the deck was dealt, how long that took, and what you chose to show and how far. It holds each card in order and why it was dealt: a stop, a meeting, a scan, a member near you, or the nearest people and places. It holds what you did with each card: whether it came up, and whether you rated it, skipped it, said "Not here" or undid it. Because a deck is made from what's near you, this record can show roughly where you were, and which members were near you, when each deck was dealt. We use it to run your deck, to improve it and to count how well it works. No other member sees it. With Places or People on, we keep it while your account exists. With both off, the cards dealt because they were nearest you are deleted after 90 days, and the rest of the record is kept while your account exists. Turning Places off deletes the cards dealt from your stops. Turning People off deletes the cards dealt from the notes it deletes, in your deck and in the other member's. What was done with each card goes with it. If you turn Meetyr's location off in iPhone's Settings, the whole record goes with your notes (see Places and People).
  • Views: when you open a discussion, tap its link or play its video, we note that you did, to count its views and rank discussions. Members see only the number, never who viewed. We keep who viewed a discussion for 8 days; after that, only the number stays.

We don't use advertising identifiers or tracking across other companies' apps and websites. meetyr.com and the web version of Meetyr count visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which uses no cookies, doesn't identify you, and doesn't follow you to other websites. The iPhone and Android apps don't use it.

Places, People and "Let businesses know when I'm there"

Meetyr asks before it notices anything. On the screen "Let Meetyr notice where you stop?", and any time in Me → Visits, you can turn on Places, People, both or neither. Both are off until you turn one on, and you never need either one to rate. Business accounts don't have them. We keep a record of each time you turn one on or off. "Let businesses know when I'm there" is a separate switch in Me → Visits, also off until you turn it on.

  • How a stop is noticed: with Places or "Let businesses know when I'm there" on and location "Always", your phone watches the 19 businesses nearest you and notices when you stay at one. Your phone does the watching. On iPhone, when you've moved about a third of a mile, your phone tells us where it is, even with Meetyr closed: we find the businesses near you and keep only that latest spot, for 24 hours at most, which also counts for crossed paths (above). On Android, when you've moved about a mile, it asks us for the businesses near its new spot; we use that location only to answer and don't store it. With Places on, three more things note a stop:
    • your iPhone tells Meetyr when you've stopped somewhere and when you leave, even with Meetyr closed;
    • with Meetyr open, on iPhone or Android, your phone stays still for a minute;
    • the latest spot your phone sent us stays put for 10 minutes.
    With location "While using", stops are noticed only while Meetyr is open.
  • Home: your phone works out where home is, from the nights it spends there and from what you tell it, and keeps your homes on your phone only. A stop within about 75 m of a home isn't noted, and nor is a long stop overnight. Until your phone knows a home, Places notes no stop. "Forget my homes" in Me → Visits clears them.
  • Places: for each stop away from home, we keep a note of:
    • when you arrived and left;
    • the exact spot of each reading your phone gives, and how precise it was;
    • a random number for the phone that reported it;
    • the places nearby, with how likely Meetyr thinks you were at each.
    Your deck shows you the places you may have been at, from the last 24 hours, and its first card may ask "How was it?", with "Not here". When you rate a place or scan its code, we also note when, and roughly where you were: an area about 28 m across, from the latest spot your phone sent us, and whether you were at home. At a place we never note, or within 565 m of a family planning center (see below), we keep neither the area nor whether you were at home. We keep your answers, the cards you skip, and what your deck has learned from them, such as the places you rate from a spot where you often stop. We also keep daily counts from your phone of how noticing went, which don't say where you were.
  • Visit notes: when you stay at a business, or a stop could be at one of a few, we also keep a visit note. It says which business, when you arrived and left, and, where it could be one of a few, the area about 30 m across where you stopped. For 24 hours a visit note counts as being there, for a business that asks to be rated nearby or in person.
  • People: we note the members who were near you, roughly where, and when, and show you to them, to rate, if they have People on too. Two members are noted near each other only when both have People on (see Crossed paths and Near a connection).
  • How long: your notes are kept until you turn that switch off, which deletes them at once, or until you delete your account.
    • Turning Places off deletes your stop notes, visit notes, the notes of where you rated, your answers, what your deck has learned, and the cards dealt from your stops. Shared sums already made stay (see Learning). Cards you skipped stay skipped.
    • Turning People off deletes your notes of who was near you, and the cards dealt from them, in your deck and in the other member's. A note that either of you rated from stays, with that rating (see Crossed paths).
    • Your ratings stay: they're what you posted.
    • "Clear my deck memory", in Me → Visits, deletes what your deck has learned, including your skips and the notes of where you rated. Your stop notes stay until you turn Places off.
  • iPhone's location setting:
    • With "While using", stops are noticed only while Meetyr is open, and nothing is deleted.
    • If you turn Meetyr's location off, the next time you open Meetyr it asks you to turn it back on. If you don't, your notes of places and people, and the record of your decks, are deleted, and Places and People are turned off. Your ratings stay.
    • If you turn Precise Location off, Meetyr asks the same way. If you don't turn it back on, we delete the exact spot of each reading in your notes, and keep each note's area, about 28 m across, and its places. While it's off, Meetyr notes no new stops.
  • Notes from before: if you turn Places on, the visit notes Meetyr already had are kept too. If you leave Places off, or don't answer, they're deleted within 2 days.
  • Who sees them: no other member sees your notes. They reach your deck only as cards, dealt from the last 24 hours. Meetyr's team reads a member's notes only through recorded requests that give a reason.
  • Learning: your notes and answers also help Meetyr guess better for everyone, with no name shown to anyone. For example, once two members have said "Not here" at a place, Meetyr stops asking "How was it?" there. Each night we also work out shared sums, from members' stop notes, what they answered and the codes they scanned, of how near a phone's location is to the place a member turns out to be at. The sums hold no member, place or location. We keep them only in that form, and don't try to find out whose notes they came from. They have no end date: when your notes are deleted, sums already made stay, and later ones leave your notes out.
  • Places we never note: sensitive places, such as health care (pharmacies included), places of worship, lawyers and courts, police stations, jails and prisons, military sites, schools and childcare, shelters, food banks and other social services, funeral homes, union offices, LGBTQ+ venues, services for immigrants, refugees and ethnic communities, dispensaries, smoke and vape shops, pawn shops, and adult venues and stores.
    • We don't note a stop when Meetyr puts the chance at 1 in 4 or more that it was at one of these. Below that, the stop is noted like any other, with its exact spot, but the note keeps nothing about such a place. In Washington, we don't note a stop when a health care place is near, and a visit note within 150 m of one is deleted within 2 days, as if Places were off.
    • These places get no visit note, reminder or arrival alert either.
    • To catch the ones Meetyr doesn't list, we keep a separate list of where they are, taken from open map data, which no one sees.
    • We also don't note a stop, a visit, or two members near each other within 565 m (1,850 feet) of a family planning center. If you scan a business's counter code there, the visit counts for rating it, and its visit note is deleted when your time to rate ends.
    • Our lists don't hold every such place, so a stop at one we don't know of is noted like any other. A note made before we knew of a place stays, unless the law requires us to delete it.
  • Reminders: with Places on, after a visit of at least 5 minutes to a business, one notification asks how it was, as before. At most one comes per business a day, and none once you've rated it (then only its question, if it asked one). While "Rate from the reminder" is on, in Me → Visits, a reminder about one business names it, on the lock screen too, and lets you rate it there. A "Remind me tomorrow" you chose still comes as before.
  • Letting businesses know: if you turn this on, a verified business on Meetyr sees your name and photo once you've been there a few minutes, if your phone's location clearly puts you at that business, and for 12 hours after. Where businesses are close together, only the one you rate after your visit sees you. It doesn't see your rating or where else you go. A verified business can also turn on arrival alerts, a notification that "A Meetyr member is here now", which never names you. Turning this off hides you from businesses right away.
  • Learning places: when you rate a business after a stop, we add one to a count for that business in that area, without keeping who rated. Once it has been rated there often enough, a stop there names it.

Helping test where Meetyr thinks you stopped

Testing is only for members we invite, and only if you say yes on the testing screen. You never need it to use Meetyr or to rate.

  • What we keep: each time you stop somewhere away from home, a note of when you arrived and left, the exact spot of each reading your phone gives and how precise it was, and the places nearby. A stop within about 75 m of your home isn't noted, and nor is a long stop overnight. Your phone works out where home is and keeps it on your phone only. If it doesn't know, the app asks you once whether you're at home, and notes nothing until it knows. Places we never note (above) applies to test notes too. With Places off, no new test notes are made, and the ones you have stay until you stop testing. When Meetyr asks Apple's maps which places are near a stop, it doesn't say who you are, and it keeps nothing of Apple's answer with your stop or your account. The one thing it keeps is Apple's reference number for a place, on that place's own listing.
  • What you see: a list of your last 3 days of stops, where you can say where you really were. We keep what you say with the note. With Places on, your deck also shows you places where you may have stopped (see Places and People).
  • Who sees them: no other member. Meetyr's team uses them to test and improve how the app works out where you stopped, and each time the team looks up a member's notes, it's recorded. That record holds the member's ID, the time and the reason, but never a place. It stays after an account is deleted, so we can check how notes were used.
  • How long: notes are kept until you stop testing or delete your account, or, if you have Places on, until you turn Places off or delete your account. If we end testing, your notes stay until you do one of those.
  • Stopping: you can stop at any time on the testing screen, and your notes are then deleted at once, unless you have Places on: then they're kept under Places, and turning Places off deletes them. Deleting your account deletes them too. We keep a record that you joined and left testing, and daily counts that don't say where you were.

MeetUp

In a MeetUp, two connections agree on a place and a time to meet. It's on iPhone only, and needs location "Always" and Precise Location.

  • Sharing your location: only during a MeetUp you both agreed to, and only with the person you're meeting. It starts when you choose, as early as 2 hours before or at 15 minutes before, and ends when you meet, when either of you stops or cancels it, or 15 minutes after the time (more if either of you adds time). Meanwhile they see where you are on the MeetUp's map, and how far away you are on their lock screen, even with Meetyr closed. Your iPhone shows that it's using your location the whole time.
  • What we keep: only your latest spot while you share, never a trail. When the MeetUp ends, we erase it and the place. Notes you write while planning go into your dialogue with them once you both agree.
  • Meeting: when your phones show you've met, the two of you can rate each other for 24 hours, and we keep where, as for a bump (above).
  • Past MeetUps: we keep who, when and whether you met, never the place. Only the two of you see them, and you can hide one from your list. Meetyr's team looks at one only after a report, or when the law requires it. If you delete your account, the other person sees "Meetyr member" instead of you.

What other members can see

  • Your display name, @handle, profile photo, person or business, category, Meetyr score and number of ratings.
  • Your "About" text, unless you hide it in Edit profile.
  • That you're nearby, never where you are. Their phone gets a rough distance to your area, which the app doesn't show. Discover shows people near the viewer as faces around the viewer's own spot, never at a place on the map, and your card can say when you were last there ("Around now", "Earlier today" or "Yesterday"). You show near a place only once you've been there 10 minutes. At home, and while "Show me near where I am" is off in Edit profile, Discover uses your home area instead of where you are.
  • During a MeetUp you both agreed to, the person you're meeting sees where you are while you share (see MeetUp).
  • Business accounts are always public. A business's About, distance and business location are shown to everyone, and a business people visit also shows its street address, with Directions. A business sets its location by entering an address or using its current location; it doesn't follow the owner's phone. Businesses that go to their customers instead appear as a rough area, like people.
  • That you rated them. When you rate someone, they see your name in their Feed, but never whether you gave a thumbs up or a thumbs down. Names show 3 people at a time, and a rating joins their score at a random time 1 to 3 hours later, or sooner together with 2 other people's ratings. A private profile shows "A private member" instead of your name. A score shows from the first rating that counts.
  • Private profiles. Private is for rating businesses, or people you meet. A person turns it on in Edit profile; it starts 24 hours later, and turning it off is immediate. While private, they rate people, and people rate them, only in person: by scanning a QR code or bumping phones. Whoever they rate sees "A private member" instead of their name, photo and profile, and ratings made while private stay that way even after it's turned off. A private member isn't shown in Discover, the map or Search, sees only businesses there, and isn't in Meetyr's public communities. When Private starts, they leave those communities, rating requests still waiting, to or from them, are withdrawn, and businesses never see them in "Here now". We can still see who made every rating, and whether it was up or down, to keep Meetyr safe.
  • Erasers. Each person (not business) has one eraser. It takes every rating they received in the last 7 days out of their score at once. We keep an erased rating on file, marked as erased. Profiles of people say that a score can leave out a week of erased ratings.
  • Requests to rate beyond a mile. If you rate a person more than 1 mile away, they're asked to confirm they had an interaction with you before the rating counts. They see your name, never your rating. Businesses don't confirm ratings.
  • Legends. Legends are historical figures and storybook characters you can rate for fun. They aren't members and can't rate anyone. When your rating of a Legend counts, your name, @handle and photo appear in its "Rated by" list, which other members can see, but never your thumb. You aren't listed if your profile was private when you rated or is private now, and members you've blocked, or who blocked you, don't see you there. A Legend's score moves right away, so someone watching it may be able to tell how you rated it. We keep each Legend rating with your account (your thumb, the question and whether your answer was right) and delete it if you delete your account.
  • Communities. A community a person starts is invitation only, and a business's community is for people who've rated it, who join from its page or with its QR code. What's in one is seen only by its members. Meetyr's public communities (USA, MeetyrMaid and your town's, which everyone starts in, and News, Sports, Rants, The Ick, Receipts and Stories, which anyone can join) are open to everyone on Meetyr except private members: everyone in one sees who's in it and what you post there, with your name and photo, and every question there is Anonymous. In a community hosted by a person, members see your name, @handle and photo, even if your profile is private, and the questions, comments, photos, videos and voice clips you add. Whoever asks a question picks Public or Anonymous, and it can't be changed after. On a Public question, the person who asked and the host see each vote as it comes in, and everyone in the community sees each vote once it closes. On an Anonymous question, no member, not even the asker or the host, sees who voted or what anyone picked, only the totals. In a business community, every question is Anonymous: the business sees your name and photo in its customer list ("A private member" if your profile is private), never how you answered, and only totals from at least 5 people once a question closes. Customers never see each other. A community's activity shows in the Feeds under the same rules. Meetyr keeps each vote with your account, Anonymous ones too; only Meetyr's team can see who voted how. Nothing in a community counts toward anyone's Meetyr score.
  • That you're visiting a business, only if you turn on "Let businesses know when I'm there" (see above).
  • If you turn on People, members near you who also have People on may get your card to rate.
  • Chats: only the people in a dialogue or group chat can see its messages, under their names, and a verified business can be one of them. You can be in a group chat with someone you're not connected to, if a connection of yours started it.
  • Rants and discussions: the members of the community you post one to see it with your name and photo, as above. A rant shows how many reacted, not who.
  • MeetyrMaid: everyone in it sees what you share, with your name, and its score once 3 people have rated it, never who rated it or how. These ratings never count toward anyone's Meetyr score.
  • Community scores: your score shows only in that community, to members who've joined in and rated you, and it never counts toward your Meetyr score. A score needs 3 ratings, so it can't show until 4 people have joined in. You rate your interaction with each person once, and it can't be changed. In community scores, Meetyr never shows who gave which thumb. The other person sees your name, not your thumb. In a private group, and especially a small one, members may be able to work out who gave which thumb from how a score moves. Meetyr's team can see who gave which thumb in community scores, and uses that only to check a report. Each check is recorded.

The app never shows anyone how a particular member rated them. Votes on a Public community question are different (see Communities above).

Businesses Meetyr lists from open data

So members can find and rate local businesses before they join, Meetyr lists some businesses itself, marked "Not on Meetyr yet". Their names, kinds of business, locations and street addresses come from open map data published by the Overture Maps Foundation, which combines data from Meta, Microsoft, Foursquare and others (see Data credits). These listings aren't connected to Meetyr or endorsed by the businesses.

  • We list storefront businesses only. We leave out homes and businesses run from home or that travel to their customers, health care, places of worship, schools and childcare, government offices, and listings that look like one person's practice.
  • We don't import phone numbers, email addresses, websites or social media accounts. A listing's street address is shown on its profile, so an owner can be sure which location is theirs.
  • Members can rate a listed business from anywhere. The first rating gives the listing an account that nobody can sign in to until its owner claims it.
  • If a business claims its listing, it sees who rated it, including before it joined (private members show as "A private member"), but never anyone's thumb.
  • To ask us to fix or remove a listing, tap the shield on its profile or email support@meetyr.com. We remove listings quickly if they're someone's home or a person rather than a business. We don't remove a listing because of its ratings.

Keeping and deleting information

We keep your information while your account exists. When you delete your account, it's deleted at once and can't be restored: your profile, photo, location, QR code, visit notes, test notes, place and people notes, the record of your decks and what your deck has learned, location settings, push tokens, and the ratings you gave and received are removed from Meetyr, and no one can see them. Other members' records of their decks keep only your account's internal number, which then names no one. If you signed in with Apple, we also end Meetyr's access to your Apple ID; if you signed in with Google, the app asks Google to end Meetyr's access. In communities, your questions (with their comments), your comments, rants, discussions and replies, the photos, videos and voice clips you added, and your invitations are deleted (the reports you made are kept for 1 year, see below), and your votes in communities hosted by a person stay only in totals, linked to no one. A community you host passes to the longest-standing member who can host it, or is deleted if nobody else is in it, and a business community is deleted with the business. A rating you gave stops counting toward another member's score at the first score update after midnight Pacific time, not at the moment you leave. Until then only the number remains, without your name. Copies may remain in our encrypted backups until we remove them. We review each copy after 90 days. We may keep limited records longer when the law requires it or to resolve a safety report. Place and people notes, visit notes among them, are kept until you turn that switch off or delete your account, and the record of your decks while your account exists, though with Places and People both off, the cards dealt because they were nearest you go after 90 days. Shared sums have no end date (see Learning). If Places is off, visit notes are deleted within 2 days. Test notes are kept until you stop testing or delete your account, or under Places while it's on, and location settings while your account exists. If we ever restore from a backup, notes, deck memory and location settings deleted since that backup was made are deleted again. To do that, we keep a record that an account was deleted, that a tester left testing, or that a member turned Places or People off or cleared their deck memory, and when, with the account's internal number and no name. The exact place of a bump, touch, scan or MeetUp meeting is deleted after 1 year, and so is the rough square of crossed paths, unless you both have People on. The Monologue ended on November 12, 2026: its entries were deleted that day and are removed for good 90 days later, unless the law requires us to keep them.

In communities, a question keeps its votes, comments and photo, video or voice clip after it closes. It's deleted when its asker or the host removes it, when the asker leaves or is removed from the community, or when the community is deleted. Your comments are also deleted when you leave or are removed from a community. When a question is removed, its photo, video or voice clip is hidden from everyone, but for now we keep the file, as we do for one that never made it onto a question; it's deleted 90 days after its owner deletes their account, or 1 year after if it was reported. A photo, video or voice clip Meetyr's team removes after a report is deleted 1 year later, unless the law requires us to keep it longer.

We keep a locked copy of your account, and of everything deleted with it, for 90 days after you delete it, so we can look into safety reports, misuse and legal requests. Notes of your stops, test notes, the record of your decks and location settings aren't in it: they're deleted at once, as is a visit note for a stop that could be at one of a few. A visit note at one business, and the notes of who was near you, are in it, except a visit note near a family planning center or, in Washington, near a health care place, which is deleted at once too. No member can see it. Only Meetyr's team can open it, only for safety or legal reasons, and each time is logged. It doesn't include your password or sign-in tokens. Reports made about you or by you, and what they're about, are kept for 1 year after you delete your account, with your name, @handle and the date you deleted it, so someone can't escape a report by deleting their account. After 90 days the rest of the copy is permanently deleted, unless the law requires us to keep it longer, for example for a police or court request. We email you when the copy is deleted.

We keep a record of each time Meetyr's team looks up a member's notes, with the member's ID, the time and the reason, never a place. It stays after an account is deleted, so we can check how notes were used.

If the law requires us to keep a member's notes for a legal demand, we keep a held copy of their notes, visit notes, notes of who was near them and the record of their decks while the demand is open, even after they turn a switch off or delete their account.

When your profile photo is changed or removed, we keep the old one locked for 1 year, then delete it. Only Meetyr's team can open it, and a police or court request or a legal hold can keep it longer.

When a business deletes its business community, we keep a locked copy of it for 90 days, or longer where the law requires, as we do for a deleted account. The same goes for a community its host deletes.

Chats have no time limit. A dialogue ends when either person leaves, and a group chat ends when fewer than 2 people are left. What was in it is then kept locked for 90 days and deleted. A reported message is kept for 1 year. When you delete your account, your messages and answers leave dialogues and group chats at once and go into the locked copy. If that leaves one other person, they keep their own messages for 90 days, and then it's deleted.

Community scores last as long as the community. You can stop any time: your score is hidden, and the thumbs you gave and got stay counted. The same goes if you leave. When a community is deleted, they go into its locked copy for 90 days, or longer where the law requires. When you delete your account, your thumbs go into its locked copy for 90 days, and leave other members' scores at the next batch of ratings.

If a business that claimed its listing deletes its account, the listing stays on Meetyr with its ratings, and support can hand it to a new owner.

When a listed business is closed, removed, or replaced because its owner chose to start fresh, we hide it and keep its ratings on file, not shown and not counted in any score, for moderation and legal records.

Security

Information is sent over encrypted connections and stored with access controls that let each member reach only their own private data. No system is perfectly secure, but we work to protect your information and will notify you if a breach affects you, as the law requires. Messages are encrypted on the way to our servers and while stored there, but they aren't end-to-end encrypted, so Meetyr's systems can read them.

Age requirement

Meetyr is for people 18 and older. On iPhone, where Apple says the law requires it, Apple asks you to share your age range before you can make an account. If you believe someone under 18 has an account, contact us and we'll delete it.
